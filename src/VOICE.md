# Voice Guide (governs all deliverable copy)

Source: PracticalConstructs/andres-portfolio (bio.html, summary.html), the closest
available authoritative voice sample. The local ResumeAutomator repo was not reachable
from this remote build environment; see ASSUMPTIONS.md.

## How Andres writes

1. **First person, active verbs.** "I build," "I ship," "I ran," "I architect." Never
   "one might consider" or passive constructions.
2. **Short declarative sentences.** One idea per sentence. Longer sentences appear only
   when listing concrete things.
3. **Concrete outcomes with numbers.** "Cut design cycle time in half." "Active across
   dozens of concurrent sites." Every capability claim gets an outcome attached.
4. **Principle, then proof.** He states a thesis plainly ("deployments fail at
   integration boundaries more than at components") and immediately backs it with what
   he built because of it.
5. **Practical over academic.** "Practical for production teams, not academic
   curiosities." Tools exist to ship, not to impress.
6. **Technical words earn their place.** A technical term appears only with what it does
   for the reader ("a constraint solver instead of spreadsheets"). Layman-first.
7. **A playful streak.** His portfolio nav is a stained-glass pizza wheel and his
   background is a building that assembles itself. Playfulness is structural, not
   jokey: the fun thing is also the useful thing. The Domino's-style tracker and the
   name Yurt 8 fit this exactly.
8. **Warm, confident, non-threatening.** He sells by showing working systems, not by
   pressuring. No hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge as filler).

## Hard rules for deliverables

- NO em dashes. Also avoid en dashes in prose; use commas, colons, periods, or "to"
  for ranges.
- Never name prior employers or their work products. Say "on live industrial programs"
  or "on hyperscale programs I've led," never the company name.
- Never use the word "proposal" in any deliverable. It is a case study.
- Layman tone throughout; technical depth lives in Show My Homework and stays readable.
- No placeholder text, ever, in anything shipped to /docs.
