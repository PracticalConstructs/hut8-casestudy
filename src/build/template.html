01 · The engine

Review. Generate. Learn. Repeat.

Three moves that feed each other, speaking one shared language, getting a little smarter on every pass. The demo above shows them working; here is what they are.

REVIEW

See everything as it happens

  • Live feeds of working Revit models, CAD files, and standardized spreadsheets
  • RFIs, submittals, and financials translated into plain English for the owner
  • Clashes surfaced as they occur, before they become change orders
  • ACC is the hub; contractors' Procore, Tableau, Power BI, and Microsoft Project connect by API
GENERATE

Design moves in seconds

  • Deterministic guardrails alternate with AI generators, gate after gate
  • Connected through MCP, the open standard that lets AI models drive real tools safely
  • Runs in a browser through Autodesk's cloud; nobody needs Revit installed
  • No third party fees for work that needs no engineer's stamp. I have run this on live programs: weeks became seconds
LEARN

Every pass sharpens the next

  • AWS Bedrock agents on S3 data lakes that grow with every review and generation
  • Knowledge gated by review before it teaches anything
  • Tied to the codes and standards Hut 8 and its vendors flag as critical
  • Grows from stakeholder input and exemplar designs fed in as standardized JSON

One shared language. All three read and write the exact same JSON: { coordinates, parameters, relationships, patterns }. Nothing gets translated between steps, so nothing gets lost between steps. Every click in the demo writes it; you can watch it happen in the data panel.

02 · Why this, why now

Built for the scale Hut 8 is already building

Hut 8 went from zero to $26.6 billion of contracted AI lease value in about a year. Yurt 8 is that same mission pointed inward: AI scaling the way Hut 8 designs, builds, and operates. Your leadership already names first principles infrastructure design as a core pillar. That is the exact filter this whole page runs on.

Projects already in flight

Yurt 8 conforms to the work, not the other way around. Existing contracts, contractors, architects, and engineers keep their tools and their workflows. We wire into what they already produce: their ACC and Procore data, their models, their schedules. The tracker lights up around work already underway, and the loop starts finding money from day one.

Projects not yet started

New work gets the full path from the first land scan: site selection scored on data, generative test fits before an architect is even engaged, end to end BIM through construction, and a working digital asset handed to operations on day one. No project is too far along to benefit, and none is too early.

One more public number worth sitting with: redesigning Beacon Point's first data hall to a new reference architecture added 57 percent more IT capacity on the same land and the same power. That is what a single great design iteration is worth at this scale. Yurt 8 exists to make that kind of move routine instead of heroic.

Build the tool, build the team, then maintain and scale both. Every month closes with one page for leadership: dollars moved, days moved, and which scenario the data came from, whether alpha, beta, or live. If a month cannot show both numbers, that month failed and I will say so plainly.

On meetings: meetings are for decisions. Status lives on the tracker, where it is always current and nobody has to perform it. If there is no decision to make, it is an email, and usually it is nothing.

On the team: start at one, me. Scale to three. Every hire is a renaissance engineer at the top of their discipline who rejects traditional role boundaries. Critical thought, delivered results, first principles, real teamwork. No idle hires, no seat fillers, ever.

Hut 8 sees the tracker, not forty modules. Every asset gets one ID for life: in the model, on the tag, in the sensor feed. The over-engineered version of that standard that never survives an American job site is exactly what this is not.

05 · Show my homework

No black boxes

Here is exactly how I made this case study, what tools did the work, and what I bring with or without them. The only thing excluded is prior employer material, which appears nowhere in this work.

How this was built

One orchestrating AI agent, directed by me, driving specialized sub agents: a research crew that pulled and cross checked Hut 8's public filings, drafting agents held to my voice rules, and adversarial reviewers that attacked every claim before it shipped. Deterministic checks gated the probabilistic steps, in alternating layers.

That is not just a build method. It is the same architecture Yurt 8 runs on. I build with the pattern I am recommending, because it works.

Take the AI away and I still build this

HTML, Python, C#, C, and the .NET framework are home turf, on top of a computer science foundation and years of boots on the ground program delivery. The AI made this faster. It did not make it possible. That distinction matters when you are deciding who to trust with a system like this.

Two days, inside my comfort zone

I produced this in two days working squarely in my lane: BIM, generative design, and program delivery. Point me at a problem outside my lane and the same financial logic arrives; it just costs more calendar. I learn fast, I stay motivated, and I stay curious. Those three traits let me wrap myself around new topics and familiar ones with equal enthusiasm, and they are among my most lucrative assets.

The 3D, today and at scale

The demo is web native 3D that ships in one file and runs anywhere, with no install and no servers. That is the right tool for owner facing delivery today. When Hut 8 wants production scale simulation, the path is NVIDIA Omniverse and the open source OpenUSD ecosystem: full physics digital twins of entire AI factories, a natural fit given Beacon Point's first hall is already designed to NVIDIA's DSX reference architecture. Nice to have later; not needed to start saving money now.
